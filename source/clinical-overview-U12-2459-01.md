# 1. PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT RATIONALE



JENTADUETO ® is a twice daily fixed dose combination product of the DPP-4 inhibitor linagliptin and the biguanide metformin hydrochlroide with single unit dose strengths of 2.5 mg/500 mg; 2.5 mg/ 850 mg and 2.5 mg/1000 mg linagliptin/metformin. It is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus when treatment with both linagliptin and metformin is appropriate, in patients inadequately controlled on metformin alone or those already being treated and well controlled with the free combination of linagliptin and metformin. JENTADUETO® is further indicated in combination with sulphonylurea (i.e. for triple combination therapy) as an adjunct to diet and exercise in patients inadequately controlled on their maximal tolerated dose of metformin and sulphonylurea.

This Clinical Overview Statement is intended to provide the rationale for the changes to the Company Core Datasheet (CCDS 0277-01). Sections were revised as appropriate, and the CCDS changes are outlined.

The new information described in this Clinical Overview concerns the sections “side effects” and “Pharmacological properties” and relates to the linagliptin component of JENTADUETO® (update is done in parallel to the respective update of the CCDS for linagliptin, e.g. marketed under the tradename TRAJENTA®; TRADJENTA®)

# 3. OVERVIEW OF CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY



## 3.1 INFORMATION REGARDING CARDIOVASCULAR RISK



This section of the CCDS has been revised in line with updated pooled cardiovascular (CV) safety analysis.

Ongoing safety assessments for phase III studies include continuous independent adjudication by the Clinical Event Committee. An updated summary of the cardiovascular safety analysis of independently adjudicated cardiovascular events from phase III clinical studies was performed in 2011 (data cut-off in 15th November 2011) to support the EU type II variation for the add-on to insulin indication of linagliptin (TRAJENTA®). This most recent update (data cut-off in 6th August 2012) is the most current analysis for inclusion into the CCDS. [U12-2369-01].



The updated prospective, meta-analysis of independently adjudicated cardiovascular events is from 17 phase III clinical studies (ranging from 12 weeks to 24 months duration) involving 9462 patients with type 2 diabetes.

The primary endpoint, the composite of the occurrence or time to first occurrence of CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke or hospitalization for unstable angina, was non-significantly lower for linagliptin versus combined active and placebo comparators [Hazard ratio 0.78 (95% confidence interval 0.55;1.12)]. In total there were 60 primary events on linagliptin and 62 on comparators.

Cardiovascular events were observed to occur at a similar rate between linagliptin and placebo [Hazard ratio 1.09 (95% confidence interval 0.68 ; 1.75)]. In total there were 43 (1.03%) primary events on linagliptin and 29 (1.35%) on placebo. Cardiovascular events were also similar between linagliptin and comparator for patients on insulin background [Hazard ratio 1.09 (95% confidence interval 0.63;1.90)]. In total there were 27 (3.33%) primary events on linagliptin and 24 (2.99%) on comparator.

To date there is no evidence for an increased CV risk for linagliptin, but the number of events in the clinical programme precludes any firm conclusions.

## 3.2 INFORMATION REGARDING THE PLASMA CONCENTRATION TIME PROFILE OF LINAGLIPTIN.



The description of the plasma concentration time profile of linagliptin was changed from “triphasic” to “at least a biphasic” to cover all linagliptin PK studies where bi- to triphasic profiles was seen.

## 3.3 INFORMATION REGARDING FASTING PLASMA GLUCOSE (FPG) VALUES.



Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) values have been inserted throughout this section, wherever it is stated that Linagliptin showed significant improvements in FPG. In addition, both SI and derived units have been added for FPG throughout (mg/dL and mmol/L).

*Post-marketing data*

Rash was defined as medical concept (selection based on reported PTs) by the following PTs: rash pustular, rash, rash generalised, rash macular, rash pruritic, skin lesion, and skin reaction were used for identification of the requested events. Data retrieved from all sources in the time period from 02 May 2011 to 02 May 2012 are discussed below.







Overall, 59 cases with the PTs rash pustular, rash, rash generalised, rash macular, rash pruritic, skin lesion, skin reaction were identified. Of 59 events 58 were non-serious and one case was serious. Of 59 cases, 1 case was reported from clinical trial and 58 cases were from spontaneous sources. Of the 58 spontaneous cases, 55 were HP confirmed. The time to onset was available in 12 cases and ranged from 1 to 419 days. The outcome of 28 events was reported as recovered, of 23 events as not reported, and of 8 events as not yet recovered.

Linagliptin was discontinued in 47 cases; in 8 cases, treatment continuation/discontinuation was unknown and in 4 cases linagliptin administration was ongoing. The resolution of the event after discontinuation of linagliptin (positive dechallenge) was reported in 20 cases. Reappearance of the rash after re-introduction of linagliptin (positive re-challenge) was reported in 2 cases. The events of rash were timely associated with generalised hypersensitivity reactions in 3 cases. In 1 case rash was associated with multiple concomitant medications and previous history of drug reactions to multiple medications. In one case, rash coincided with seasonal allergies.

Hypersensitivity – of which rash is a possible manifestations – is a listed event for linagliptin. Of 59 events, 58 were non-serious. Positive re-challenge was reported in 2 cases. The reported ADRs of this SOC with respect to ‘rash’ are considered to be a manifestation of ‘hypersensitivity’ which is listed for linagliptin. For further details, please refer to [U121697-01].

Rash can be considered as being covered by the hypersensitivity label and rash was not specifically identified in the clinical trial data set SAF-2. However, due to the nature of the post-marketing cases (one case was serious, 2 cases were reported with positive re-challenge) the term will be added to the CCDS as further specification of the listed term hypersensitivity.

# 6. BENEFITS AND RISKS CONCLUSIONS



Based on the available post-marketing data, ‘rash’ is proposed as a side effects potentially associated with the use of linagliptin and is proposed to be added to the section ‘Side effects’ of the Company Core Data Sheet.



To date there is no change in the risk benefit profile for linagliptin with regards to CV risk. There is no evidence for an increased CV risk, but the number of events in the clinical programme precludes any firm conclusions. It is proposed to update the cardiovascular risk part of the section ‘Pharmacological Properties’ of the Company Core Data Sheet.
