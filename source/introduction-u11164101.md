# 1. INTRODUCTION



The combination linagliptin/metformin combines linagliptin - which is currently undergoing CHMP review - and the marketed drug metformin. [Additional information regarding linagliptin in comparison to the linagliptin mono submission is highlighted in yellow.]{.mark}

Linagliptin is a potent and selective Dipeptidyl Peptidase 4 (DPP-4) inhibitor. The improvement in glycemic control seen after administration of linagliptin is mediated by effectively prolonging the half-life and thereby increasing the plasma concentrations of endogenous incretins such as Glucagon-like Peptide-1 (GLP-1). These incretins are released by the intestine in response to a meal and are involved in the physiological regulation of glucose homeostasis. In more detail, elevated incretin hormone levels increase insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells and lower glucagon release from pancreatic alpha cells.

Metformin belongs structurally to the biguanides and is an insulin sensitizing drug, which improves fasted and postprandial plasma glucose in patients with type 2 diabetes. The mechanism of action of metformin is still not fully elucidated, however its anti-diabetic action is based on decreased hepatic glucose production via suppressed gluconeogenesis, decreased absorption of glucose from the intestine and improved insulin sensitivity. In addition, metformin was shown to increase GLP-1 release in obese patients with or without type 2 diabetes. The pharmacological action of metformin has minor or no effect on insulin secretion, but reduces the demand of insulin due to its insulin sensitizing effect by increasing peripheral uptake of glucose. The pharmacological actions of metformin are, therefore, targeted towards the liver, the muscle and the adipose tissue. Further, metformin is not associated with an increased risk of hypoglycemic episodes.

The combination of the glucose-dependent insulin secretagogue linagliptin with the insulin sensitizing drug metformin is considered an ideal principle for diabetes therapy in patients with type 2 diabetes. Phamacodynamic effects of the linagliptin/metformin combination were studied in a mouse disease model for diabetes (diabetic db/db mice). Combination toxicity studies in the rat up to 13 weeks (tested drug/drug dose ratios: 1:200 and 1:400) were performed as bridging studies to the complete nonclinical development program conducted with linagliptin. These rat studies were also used for pharmacokinetic investigations of the combination. In addition, embryo-fetal development studies in the rat were performed with metformin and with the linagliptin/metformin combination.

The exposure multiples and safety margins to clinical use indicated in this document were calculated on the base of plasma AUC. The Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD) for linagliptin is 5 mg per day and is associated with an AUC0-24h,ss of 158 nM·h. The MRHD for metformin is 2000 mg per day and is associated with an AUC0-24h,ss of 159 µM·h (=20.5 µg.h/mL).
