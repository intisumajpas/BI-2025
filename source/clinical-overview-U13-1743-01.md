1. PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT RATIONALE

## 1.1 INTRODUCTION AND TARGET INDICATION

In August 2011, linagliptin (EU trade name: Trajenta®) film-coated tablets 5 mg were approved in the European Union (EU/1/11/707) for the treatment of adult patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. Approval of an indication extension was granted in October 2012 for the use of linagliptin in combination with insulin when insulin and metformin do not provide adequate glycaemic control.
In July 2012, a fixed-dose combination of linagliptin and metformin (EU trade name: Jentadueto®) film-coated tablets (2.5 mg linagliptin / 850 mg metformin bid and 2.5 mg linagliptin / 1000 mg metformin bid) was approved in the European Union (EU/1/12/780) for the treatment of adult patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The approval was granted for the fixed-dose combination of linagliptin and metformin in patients inadequately controlled on their maximal tolerated dose of metformin alone or in patients already treated with linagliptin and metformin. The fixed-dose combination of linagliptin and metformin is additionally indicated as combination therapy with a sulphonylurea in patients inadequately controlled on their maximal tolerated dose of metformin and a sulphonylurea.
With the present submission, Boehringer Ingelheim is applying for the use of Jentadueto as combination therapy with insulin in adult patients with type 2 diabetes when insulin and metformin do not provide adequate glycaemic control.
## 1.2 MEDICAL BACKGROUND AND DRUG PROFILE

The current estimated worldwide prevalence of diabetes is 366 million with an increase of 50% expected within the next 20 years [R12-1019]. Type 2 diabetes mellitus is the most common type of diabetes and is characterised by multiple metabolic abnormalities involving insulin resistance, impaired insulin secretion, and increased glucose production. Patients with type 2 diabetes are at increased risk of developing macrovascular complications such as cardiovascular disease and microvascular complications such as retinopathy, neuropathy, and nephropathy [R04-2186].
Treatment of patients with type 2 diabetes usually involves lifestyle interventions such as diet and exercise as well as the intake of oral antidiabetic agents. Although initially effective, traditional oral antidiabetic agents often fail in the long-term maintenance of glycaemic control, apparently due to a progressive loss of β-cell function. In many cases, the addition of insulin therapy is needed to maintain adequate glycaemic control. Therefore, patients treated with insulin therapy alone or combined with oral antidiabetic drugs represent an important proportion of the patient population with type 2 diabetes. Over time, however, a substantial proportion of patients taking insulin are unable to maintain adequate glycaemic control [R11-4654]. Although increasing the insulin dose may be an option to improve glycaemic control, this presents an increased risk of hypoglycaemia [R11-4273] and weight gain [R11-4659]. Patients may therefore benefit from additional oral antidiabetic drugs that can be administered with their background insulin therapy.
Linagliptin is a selective, orally administered, xanthine-based DPP-4 inhibitor discovered by Boehringer Ingelheim. It lowers blood glucose by extending the half-life of glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1), which is secreted in response to a meal [P08-07949]. GLP-1 lowers blood glucose by augmenting the glucose-stimulated insulin release and limiting glucagon secretion to slow gastric emptying and to induce satiety. Because GLP-1 activity ceases when plasma glucose concentration falls below 55 mg/dL, prolongation of the half-life of GLP-1 by DPP-4 inhibitors bears little risk of hypoglycaemia.
Metformin has been used in Europe for over 50 years and lowers blood glucose levels primarily by suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis. It also improves the insulin sensitivity of peripheral tissues and decreases gastrointestinal tract glucose absorption but does not exert any direct effect on pancreatic β-cell insulin secretion. Through these mechanisms, metformin therapy reduces HbA~1c~ but does not promote weight gain or increase the risk of hypoglycaemia [R10-4882, R10-4896].
The combination of linagliptin with metformin provides clinically meaningful treatment benefits by lowering glucose and reducing glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA~1c~) further than monotherapy with either component at corresponding doses. Combining linagliptin with metformin in a fixed dose combination tablet simplifies the antidiabetic therapy by decreasing the number of tablets to be taken and should improve the adherence of patients with their antidiabetic treatment.
## 1.3 CLINICAL DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME

Clinical development of linagliptin (Trajenta®) started in October 2004. The clinical programme that formed the basis for the initial application for marketing authorisation of linagliptin as monotherapy comprised 24 Phase I trials (among them 9 drug-drug interaction trials), 4 Phase II trials, and 9 large Phase III trials including an open-label extension trial of the 4 pivotal placebo-controlled trials. The clinical development programme that formed the basis for the application for marketing authorisation of the fixed-dose combination of linagliptin and metformin (Jentadueto®) began in September 2005 and comprised 13 clinical trials, several of which were also part of the linagliptin monotherapy program. No dedicated clinical programme for Jentadueto as add-on therapy to insulin was conducted. This application comprises results from three completed Phase III trials that evaluated the efficacy and safety of linagliptin. Each of these trials included subsets of patients who were taking both metformin and insulin as background medication; only these groups of patients are included in the current application.
The main proof of efficacy of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin is provided by patients from the double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III trial 1218.36, which investigated the efficacy and safety of linagliptin 5 mg once daily versus placebo as add-on therapy to basal insulin over at least 52 weeks. The primary endpoint was the HbA~1c~ change from baseline after 24 weeks and was initially evaluated in an interim analysis [U11-2286] which was conducted to support the application for the extension of the indication for linagliptin to include add-on insulin. The final report [U12-1511] includes data over the entire study duration, including the primary endpoint analysis. Data from the final analysis of trial 1218.36 are presented in this clinical overview. Only those patients from that trial who were taking metformin as background medication (in addition to insulin) are included here.
Supportive data are available from two additional Phase III trials that included subsets of patients who took linagliptin 5 mg and placebo in addition to background metformin and insulin therapy. Trial 1218.43 [U11-3170] compared linagliptin 5 mg once daily with placebo over 52 weeks as add-on to pre-existing antidiabetic therapy (including insulin and/or other antidiabetic drugs) in patients with type 2 diabetes and severe chronic renal impairment. Patients were included in trial 1218.43 if their estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) was less than 30 mL/min (as calculated with the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease [MDRD] formula). Although metformin is contraindicated in such patients, trial 1218.43 nevertheless included 3 patients taking metformin and insulin as background therapy; these patients were included in the analysis of safety in the current submission. Trial 1218.63 [U11-1781] was a 24-week study that investigated the efficacy and safety of linagliptin 5 mg versus placebo in elderly patients (aged 70 years or older) with type 2 diabetes and insufficient glycaemic control despite treatment with metformin and/or SU and/or insulin.
All three trials were conducted according to ICH GCP guidelines subsequent to review and approval from the relevant ethics committees, institutional review boards, and regulatory authorities. They were randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group studies and were conducted in male and female patients with type 2 diabetes whose blood concentrations of HbA~1c~ indicated inadequate glycaemic control. Following the database lock for the final analyses of trials 1218.36 and 1218.43, a study site that contributed 15 screened patients to trial 1218.36 and 13 screened patients to study 1218.43 was identified with serious non-compliance issues. The sponsor decided that the clinical trial reports would not be revised to exclude these patients but that the patients are not be included in further analyses, including the analyses included in this submission. Hence, all 28 patients were excluded from the screened set for this submission. From the patients who would have been eligible for inclusion in this submission (i.e. patients who were taking both metformin and insulin as background medication), a total of 6 treated patients (3 patients treated with linagliptin and 3 patients treated with placebo) were therefore excluded.
For the integrated analyses presented within this submission, the nomenclature of the treatment groups differs from that on trial level (i.e. within the clinical trial reports). The placebo group on trial level is denoted as the 'placebo+metformin+insulin' group within this document and the linagliptin group on trial level is denoted as the 'linagliptin+metformin+insulin' group within this document.

2. OVERVIEW OF BIOPHARMACEUTICS

For the establishment of efficacy and safety, a film-coated tablet containing 5 mg of linagliptin was developed and characterised in regard to its pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics. Evidence of its clinical efficacy and safety was provided by the development programme for linagliptin monotherapy. For the fixed-dose combination, the therapeutic dose of linagliptin 5 mg once daily was split into two doses of 2.5 mg each in order to conform to the twice daily dosing required for metformin. Evidence of the bioequivalence between the fixed-dose formulations and the free combinations was established during the clinical development programme for Jentadueto.

3. OVERVIEW OF CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY

During the initial linagliptin clinical development programme, the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of linagliptin were evaluated in healthy subjects and in patients with type 2 diabetes. In these studies, linagliptin was administered as a single oral dose up to 600 mg or multiple doses up to 10 mg per day every 24 hours. The studies were designed to address issues related to linagliptin single-dose and multiple-dose pharmacokinetics, dose proportionality, excretion/mass balance, metabolite identification, effects of food on bioavailability, pharmacodynamics, drug interactions, pharmacokinetics in special populations (gender, race, BMI/weight, elderly, and renal or hepatic impairment), and absolute/relative bioavailability. The most relevant results from these studies are summarised below; additional information is available in the Summary of Product Characteristics [R13-2104]. With regard to the current submission, trough levels of linagliptin were reported in studies 1218.43 [U11-3170] and 1218.63 [U11-1781] and were found to be consistent with findings from previous studies. Trough levels of linagliptin were also evaluated in the pivotal trial 1218.36 and are provided in the final report [U12-1511].
There is no need for dose adjustment of linagliptin on the basis of age, gender, BMI, race, or renal or hepatic impairment. Moreover, linagliptin had no clinically relevant effect on the pharmacokinetics of metformin, glibenclamide, simvastatin, pioglitazone, warfarin, digoxin, or oral contraceptives. These findings constitute in vivo evidence of a low propensity for drug interactions with substrates of CYP-3A4, CYP-2C9, CYP-2C8, P-glycoprotein (P-gp), and organic cationic transporters.
Co-administration of a single 5 mg oral dose of linagliptin and multiple 200 mg oral doses of ritonavir, a potent inhibitor of P-gp and CYP-3A4, increased the AUC and Cmax of linagliptin approximately 2-fold and 3-fold, respectively. The concentrations of unbound linagliptin, which are usually less than 1% of total linagliptin concentrations at the therapeutic dose of linagliptin, were increased 4-fold to 5-fold after co-administration with ritonavir. Nevertheless, simulations of steady-state plasma concentrations of linagliptin with and without ritonavir indicated that the increase in exposure will not be associated with an increased accumulation. These changes in linagliptin pharmacokinetics were not considered to be clinically relevant. Since ritonavir is one of the most potent inhibitors of P-gp/CYP-3A4, clinically relevant interactions of linagliptin with other P-gp/CYP-3A4 inhibitors are not expected.
Conversely, multiple co-administrations of linagliptin with rifampicin, a potent inducer of P-gp and CYP-3A4, resulted in a decrease of about 40% in linagliptin steady-state exposure and a decrease of about 30% in DPP-4 inhibition. This observation is most likely due to an inductive effect on P-gp. Thus, full efficacy of linagliptin in combination with strong P-gp inducers might not be achieved, particularly if these are administered long-term. Coadministration with other potent inducers of P-gp and CYP-3A4, such as carbamazepine, phenobarbital, and phenytoin has not been studied.
No dedicated studies were performed that investigated the effect of intrinsic or extrinsic factors on the pharmacokinetics of the linagliptin/metformin fixed-dose combination formulation. However, it can be assumed that the same conditions, limitations, and dose adjustments as reported for the single entities will apply to the fixed-dose single-tablet combination. Hence, for the linagliptin/metformin fixed-dose combination, no dose adjustment is necessary based on age, gender, BMI/weight, or race. However, due to the metformin component, treatment with the combination should generally be avoided in patients with hepatic impairment because of a possible association between hepatic impairment and lactic acidosis. The fixed-dose combination is also contraindicated in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <60 mL/min).

4. OVERVIEW OF EFFICACY

## 4.1 ANALYSIS OF EFFICACY

Two Phase III trials (1218.36 and 1218.63) contribute to the proof of efficacy of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin. The trials were arranged into two relevant efficacy groupings (EFF-1 and EFF-2) and include subsets of patients who were treated with both metformin and insulin as background therapy.

The principal proof of efficacy of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin is based on EFF-1, which comprises a subset of patients from the pivotal placebo-controlled trial 1218.36, which investigated the efficacy of linagliptin versus placebo as add-on therapy to basal insulin with or without oral antidiabetic drugs. All patients from trial 1218.36 who were taking both metformin and insulin as background therapy (comprising 82.9% of patients in 1218.36) are included in EFF-1. The primary endpoint was the HbA~1c~ change from baseline after 24 weeks; efficacy was also investigated over the entire study duration of at least 52 weeks.
Efficacy in elderly patients is based on EFF-2, which comprises a pooling of elderly patients (≥70 years) from trials 1218.36 (insulin add-on trial) and 1218.63 (trial conducted in elderly patients) who were taking both metformin and insulin as background therapy. The primary endpoint was the HbA~1c~ change from baseline after 24 weeks.

### 4.2.4 Background insulin at screening

All patients included in the efficacy study groupings for this submission were treated with insulin as background therapy. A prerequisite for participation in trial 1218.36, which provided patients to EFF-1, was that patients were treated with basal insulin. Almost half of the patients in EFF-1 (48.6%) were taking insulin glargine as their basal insulin therapy, whereas 31.4% were taking Neutral Protamine Hagedorn (NPH) insulin and 20.0% were taking insulin detemir. The average mean daily dose of insulin in EFF-2 was 33.7 IU, and was similar in both treatment groups. Further details on background insulin at screening are available in the SCE [Module 2.7.3, Section 3.1.3.2.1].

### 4.3.2 Categorical efficacy analyses: proportions of patients achieving HbA~1c~ below 7.0% or HbA~1c~ lowering by at least 0.5%

The number of patients who reached HbA~1c~ values of <7.0% and the number who had their HbA~1c~ levels lowered by 0.5% or more was analysed for each of the efficacy groupings, with a higher proportion of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group achieving these HbA~1c~ responses.
Of the patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36 (EFF-1) who had baseline HbA~1c~ of 7.0% or greater, 21.2% of those in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 8.4% of those in the placebo+metformin+insulin group achieved HbA~1c~ values of less than 7.0% after 24 weeks of treatment. After 52 weeks, which included the time during which the insulin dose could be adjusted, 17.0% of the patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 6.4% of the patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group achieved HbA~1c~ values of less than 7.0%.  The proportion of patients that had a reduction in HbA~1c~ of at least 0.5% after 24 weeks was higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (56.5%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (22.5%). After 52 weeks, 40.0% of the patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 17.0% of the patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group achieved HbA~1c~ reductions of at least 0.5%.
Of the elderly patients (EFF-2) who had baseline HbA~1c~ of 7.0% or greater, 30.0% of those in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 6.9% of those in the placebo+metformin+insulin group achieved HbA~1c~ values of less than 7.0% after 24 weeks of treatment. The proportion of patients that had a reduction in HbA~1c~ of at least 0.5% after 24 weeks was 62.4% in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 15.6% in the placebo+metformin+insulin group.

## 4.4 CHANGE FROM BASELINE IN FPG

The change from baseline in FPG was a secondary endpoint in both trials included in the analysis of efficacy for this submission. For patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36 (EFF-1) and the grouping of elderly patients (EFF-2), the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin was superior to the combination of placebo, metformin, and insulin with regard to the FPG change from baseline after 24 weeks. In EFF-1 (patients taking both metformin and insulin from the placebo-controlled pivotal trial 1218.36), the adjusted mean difference between treatment groups with regard to the FPG change from baseline was -12.5 mg/dL (95% CI: -17.8, -7.2; p<0.0001). For the grouping of elderly patients (EFF-2), the adjusted mean treatment difference was -19.5 mg/dL (95% CI: -33.4, -5.6; p=0.0061), which is in line with the results for HbA~1c~. These results are consistent with findings from previous trials in the linagliptin development program.

## 4.5 USE OF RESCUE MEDICATION

As an additional measure of efficacy, it was analysed how many patients in each efficacy grouping required rescue medication. Due to their study designs, there were slight differences in the definitions of rescue medication in each of the trials contributing patients to the efficacy analysis. In 1218.36, the first choice of rescue medication was the adjustment of basal insulin therapy; any increase in prescribed insulin dose of more than 10% of the baseline dose was regarded as rescue medication. In very rare cases, background therapy could be adjusted or another oral antidiabetic medication could be added as rescue medication. In 1218.63, rescue medication was considered as any addition of an antidiabetic drug or increase in the dose of background medication for more than 7 days.
For both of the efficacy groupings, the use of rescue medication was consistently lower for patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than for patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. For the patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36 (EFF-1), 34.4% of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group required rescue therapy, compared with 48.6% in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. Of the grouping of elderly patients (EFF-2), 6.5% in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group required rescue medication, compared with 17.8% in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. For details on the definitions of rescue medication in the individual trials, refer to the SCE [Module 2.7.3, Section 1.2.2].

## 4.6 CHANGE FROM BASELINE IN INSULIN DOSE

According to the trial protocols, the background insulin dose was to have been maintained at a stable level up to 24 weeks for 1218.36 and 12 weeks for 1218.63. Analysis of the mean changes in insulin dose in the efficacy groupings showed that mean insulin doses were stable up to the timepoints of the primary analysis. For each of the efficacy groupings a smaller proportion of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group had insulin dose increases of more than 10% during the period up to the primary endpoint. For the patients from the placebo-controlled pivotal trial (EFF-1), the changes in insulin dose were analysed over time including the period after 24 weeks during which changes in insulin dose were permitted. During this period, the changes in mean basal insulin dose were greater for patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group; the changes from baseline at 52 weeks were 4.4 IU (from a baseline of 39.2 IU) in the placebo+metformin+insulin group and 2.4 IU (from a baseline of 40.2 IU) in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. Thus, the treatment effect in terms of difference in adjusted mean HbA~1c~ was largely maintained over time despite the higher increase in insulin dose in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. Further details on insulin dose changes are available in the SCE [Module 2.7.3, Sections 3.2.1.4 and 3.2.2.4].

## 4.7 CHANGE FROM BASELINE IN BODY WEIGHT

In each of the efficacy groupings, there were no clinically meaningful effects on body weight in both treatment groups (no more than ±1 kg change in adjusted mean weight from baseline) at the timepoint of the primary analysis at 24 weeks. For the patients from the pivotal placebo-controlled trial (EFF-1), there was no weight change in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (-0.07 kg) and slight weight gain in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (0.40 kg). These results, observed while the background insulin dose was stable, confirm that treatment with linagliptin is weight neutral. The changes in body weight from baseline at 52 weeks were negligible for both treatment groups in EFF-1. Further details are available in the SCE [Module 2.7.3, Sections 3.2.1.5 and 3.2.2.5].

## 4.8 LONG-TERM EFFECTS

The persistence of the efficacy of linagliptin as monotherapy and in combination with metformin over at least 52 weeks has already been established in clinical trials. The activecontrolled study 1218.20 [U11-1485] demonstrated sustained efficacy of linagliptin in terms of HbA~1c~ reduction and non-inferiority versus glimepiride over 104 weeks. Study 1218.40 [U11-1708] was an open-label extension of the 4 pivotal placebo-controlled trials for linagliptin monotherapy, in which the efficacy of linagliptin 5 mg once daily was investigated as monotherapy (1218.16), in combination with pioglitazone (1218.15), and in addition to metformin (1218.17) and metformin and sulphonylurea (1218.18) as background therapy [U10-1103, U09-2519, U09-2533, and U09-2458]. For the patients who had been randomised to linagliptin in the pivotal trials, the reduction in HbA~1c~ was maintained during the 78-week duration of the extension trial. Study 1218.46 [U10-2372] and its extension trial 1218.52 [U11-1782] demonstrated the persistence of the efficacy of combined treatment with linagliptin and metformin over up to 78 weeks. Detailed information on the persistence of efficacy in these trials is provided in the individual clinical trial reports.
With regard to the current submission, evidence of the persistence of the efficacy of linagliptin in combination with insulin and metformin over time is available for EFF-1 (i.e. patients from the pivotal placebo-controlled trial 1218.36 who were taking both metformin and insulin as background therapy), including the period of variable insulin dosing. For further details, refer to Section 4.3.1.2.

5. OVERVIEW OF SAFETY

## 5.1 ANALYSIS OF SAFETY

This application is supported by safety data from three Phase III trials: 1218.36, 1218.43, and 1218.63. All studies are randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies investigating linagliptin 5 mg once daily compared with placebo in patients with type 2 diabetes as add-on therapy to a background of basal insulin with or without oral antidiabetic drugs.
For the integrated analysis of safety, data from these trials were pooled in a single safety grouping. This grouping includes data from the subset of patients who were taking metformin and insulin as background medication. In total, 1079 treated patients are included in the safety analysis presented in this document with the main contribution of 1040 patients arising from study 1218.36. Of the treated patients in the safety grouping, 530 patients were in the placebo+metformin+insulin group and 549 patients were in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group.
The treated set (TS) was used for the analysis of safety and comprised all patients who received at least one dose of study medication. Concomitant therapies were coded using the World Health Organization (WHO) Drug Dictionary, version 12.Sep. Concomitant diagnoses and adverse events were coded using the MedDRA coding dictionary, version 15.1.

## 5.2 EXTENT OF EXPOSURE AND CHARACTERISATION OF THE PATIENT POPULATION

### 5.2.1 Exposure to trial medication

Planned treatment durations were at least 52 weeks for study 1218.36, 52 weeks for study 1218.43, and 24 weeks for study 1218.63. Hence, nearly all patients included in this analysis were treated for 24 weeks or more (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 93.6%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 90.9%); the majority of patients was treated for 52 weeks or more (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 82.3%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 77.7%). As in study 1218.36 treatment beyond Week 52 was allowed, a considerable proportion of patients in the safety grouping was treated for 88 weeks or more (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 8.4%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 10.6%). Thus, a meaningful number of patients was exposed to long-term treatment with linagliptin on a background of metformin and insulin, exceeding the recommendation provided in ICH E1 [R13-0028].
Exposure was generally comparable between the treatment groups; the overall mean (SD) treatment exposure was 433 (141.2) days for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 421 (153.7) days for the placebo+metformin+insulin group, and the total exposure to randomised study medication was 651.3 patient years in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 610.8 patient years in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. For further details on patient exposure, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.2.2].

### 5.2.2 Patient disposition and premature discontinuation

In the safety set, the frequency of patients who prematurely discontinued treatment was lower for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (12.6%) than for the placebo+metformin+insulin group (16.8%). Consistently, the most frequent reasons for premature treatment discontinuation were reported more often in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group; these reasons were the occurrence of adverse events (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 4.6%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 5.3%), and refusal to continue study medication (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 2.9%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 4.5%).
Consistent with the overall analysis, for most subgroups analysed, the proportion of patients who prematurely discontinued treatment was lower for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than for the placebo+metformin+insulin group; this was consistent for the age categories (≤50, 51 to <65, 65 to <75, 75 to <85, ≥85 years of age), gender subgroups (male, female), baseline BMI categories (<30, ≥30 kg/m2), baseline HbA~1c~ categories (<8.5%, ≥8.5%), renal function subgroups (≥90, <90 mL/min), and baseline insulin daily dose categories (≤40, >40 IU). Exceptions were noted for the subgroup of Black patients (N= 46), where the rate of premature treatment discontinuation was higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (34.8%) when compared with the placebo+metformin+insulin group (17.4%); the difference arose solely due to 2 patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group who refused to continue trial medication and 2 patients who were lost to follow up. There was no difference between the treatment groups in the frequency of Black patients who discontinued due to adverse events. Further subgroups with a slightly higher treatment discontinuation rate in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were the subgroup of Asian patients, patients from Asia, and patients who were diagnosed with diabetes for more than 1 and up to 5 years.
For further details on patient disposition in the safety grouping and its subgroups, refer to SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.2.1.1 and 1.2.1.2].

### 5.2.3 Demographics and baseline characteristic

Demographic data were generally balanced across the two treatment groups for the overall safety grouping and all analysed subgroups. Overall, 51.9% of patients in the safety grouping were male, 48.1% were female. The majority of patients were White (82.6%); 13.2% were Asian and 4.3% were Black. The mean (SD) age of the patient population was 60.1 (9.9) years. At baseline the patients had a mean (SD) weight of 86.22 (17.90) kg and a mean BMI of 31.14 (5.13) kg/m2. Most patients in the safety grouping (64.4%) had normal renal function (eCcr ≥90 mL/min) at baseline according to Cockcroft-Gault; 29.2% of patients had mild renal impairment (eCcr ≥60 and <90 mL/min) and 6.4% of patients had moderate renal impairment (eCcr ≥30 and <60 mL/min). This is a distribution that can be expected for a patient population treated with metformin due to respective restrictions and contraindications for the use of metformin in patients with renal impairment.
For further details on patient demographics in the safety grouping and its subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.3.1.1 and 1.3.1.2].

Adverse event analyses are based on the number of patients with adverse events, not the number of adverse events.
The types of the adverse events analysed for the SCS include all adverse events, all adverse events by intensity, investigator-defined drug-related adverse events, adverse events leading to discontinuation of study medication, adverse events leading to death, serious adverse events, other significant adverse events (based on the ICH E3 definition), hypoglycaemic events, and 8 categories of adverse events of special interest. Details on the definitions, data collection processes, and planned analyses of the different types of adverse events are described in the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.1.3.2].

Serious adverse events were slightly more frequent in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. Investigator-defined drug-related adverse events were more common in the placebo+metformin+insulin group when compared with the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. ### 5.3.2 Drug-related adverse events and adverse events leading to discontinuation

Overall, proportions of patients reported with adverse events leading to premature treatment discontinuation were low in the safety grouping and similar between the treatment groups (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 23 patients [4.2%], placebo+metformin+insulin: 25 patients [4.7%]). Generally, for all SOCs, proportions of patients with adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation were below 1.0% in both treatment groups; except for the SOC gastrointestinal disorders, which was reported for more patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (7 patients [1.3%]) than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (3 patients [0.5%]). Only a few preferred terms were reported by 2 patients in a treatment group. There was no indication that treatment with linagliptin on a background of metformin and insulin increased the rate of adverse events leading to premature discontinuation when compared with placebo treatment on a background of metformin and insulin. For further details on adverse events leading to premature treatment discontinuation, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.1.3].
The proportion of patients with investigator-defined drug-related adverse events in the safety grouping was higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (21.5%) than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (18.2%). The SOC most frequently reported for investigator-defined drug-related adverse events was metabolism and nutrition disorders (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 13.3%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 15.1%). The most frequently reported drug-related adverse event at PT level was hypoglycaemia with a higher frequency in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (75 patients [14.2%]) than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (69 patients [12.6%]). Hence, treatment with linagliptin compared with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin did not increase the incidence of drug-related adverse events in the investigated patient population. For further details on investigator-defined drug-related adverse events, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.1.4].

### 5.3.3 Serious adverse events

#### 5.3.3.1 Deaths

Of the 1079 treated patients in this safety analysis, 6 patients died; 3 patients were in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, 3 patients were in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. All fatal events were reported in study 1218.36, which contributed most patients to the safety grouping (1040 of 1079 treated patients). Of the patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group who died, 2 patients were reported with sudden death, 1 patient with myocardial infarction. Of the patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group who died, 2 patients were reported with non-small cell lung cancer, and 1 patient was reported with acute renal failure. All patients died in the on-treatment period. None of the events was considered related to trial medication. The incidence rates per 100 patient years were similar in the treatment groups: 4.53 for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and 4.83 for the placebo+metformin+insulin group. Hence, there was no indication for an increased risk for a fatal adverse event when treating patients with linagliptin compared with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin. For further details on fatal adverse events, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.2].

#### 5.3.3.2 Most frequently reported serious adverse events

Overall, in the analysed patient population, the frequency of serious adverse events (including fatal adverse events) was low. In the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group 13.8% of patients and 12.8% of patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were reported with serious adverse events. The SOC with the highest frequency of serious adverse events was cardiac disorders, reported for similar frequencies of patients in both treatment groups (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 2.9%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 2.8%). On the PT level, no event was reported with a frequency of more than 1.0% .
Based on preferred terms, serious adverse events with an incidence of more than 0.2% (i.e.  1 or more patients) in either treatment group and reported for more patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than the placebo+metformin+insulin group were angina unstable (0.5% vs. 0.2%), acute myocardial infarction (0.4% vs. 0.0%), cataract (0.4% vs. 0.0%), gastric ulcer (0.4% vs. 0.2%), sudden death (0.4% vs. 0.0%), bronchitis (0.4% vs. 0.0%), fall (0.5% vs. 0.4%), contusion (0.4% vs. 0.0%), ligament sprain (0.4% vs. 0.2%), road traffic accident (0.4% vs. 0.2%), hypoglycaemia (0.4% vs. 0.0%), ovarian adenoma (0.4% vs. 0.2%), and nephrolithiasis (0.4% vs. 0.0%). For further details on serious adverse events, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.3].

### 5.3.4 Hypoglycaemic events

In the Phase III studies contributing to the safety analysis, the investigator was to record for each adverse event whether it represented a hypoglycaemic event, and if so, additional information to assess the intensity of the event. Based on this information the hypoglycaemic events were classified as asymptomatic hypoglycaemia, mild hypoglycaemia, moderate hypoglycaemia, or severe hypoglycaemic episode, as detailed in the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.1.3.2]. Additionally, a special search category (SSC) was used to identify hypoglycaemic events; this SSC consisted of the HLT 'hypoglycaemic conditions NEC' and the MedDRA PT 'blood glucose decreased'.
The frequency of reported on-treatment hypoglycaemic events analysed by SSC and by investigator-reporting was similar in both treatment groups, ranging from 29.1% to 30.9% of patients. Hence, there was no evidence that treatment with linagliptin compared with placebo in patients with metformin and insulin background led to an increase in hypoglycaemic events. Consistently, similar proportions of patients were reported with investigator-defined hypoglycaemic events in both treatment groups irrespective of severity and also for severe hypoglycaemic events, i.e. those requiring assistance (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 1.5%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 0.9%).
In the study which contributed the majority of patients to this safety analysis (1218.36), hypoglycaemic events were analysed and compared for the first 24 weeks of treatment, where the basal insulin dose was to remain stable, and for the following period up to the end of treatment, where the basal insulin dose could be adjusted according to the medical judgement of the investigator. Overall, there was no difference between the placebo and linagliptin treatment groups for the proportion of patients with investigator-reported hypoglycaemic events up to Week 24 and after Week 24 to the end of treatment [U12-1511].
For further details on hypoglycaemic events, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.5.1].

### 5.3.5 Adverse events of special interest

Based on scientific considerations and regulatory advice, the safety grouping was searched for adverse events of special interest. Eight categories of adverse event of special interest were defined: hepatic injury, decreased renal function, hypersensitivity reactions (including ’anaphylactic reaction’, ’angioedema’, and ’asthma bronchospasm’), cutaneous skin reactions, pancreatitis, pancreatic cancer, thyroid-related safety (including ’thyroid neoplasms malignant’, ’thyroid neoplasms benign’, and ’thyroid neoplasm (unspecified)’), and drugrelated gastrointestinal adverse events (including ’vomiting’, ’nausea’, ’diarrhoea’, and ’abdominal pain’). The analysis of these adverse events was based on standardised MedDRA queries (SMQs), BI-customised MedDRA queries (BIcMQ; used only where no appropriate SMQ was available), and clinical laboratory results. For further details on the analysis methods for adverse events of special interest, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.1.3.2].
Overall, proportions of patients with adverse events of special interest were low for all types of adverse events of special interest. No patients were reported with pancreatic cancer, thyroid cancer, or thyroid neoplasm (unspecified). The adverse events of special interest reported with the highest frequency was hepatic adverse events; the frequency was slightly higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (2.9%) when compared with the placebo+metformin+insulin group (1.5%). Also slightly more patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were reported with hypersensitivity adverse events (1.8% vs.1.3%) mainly due to urticaria (0.7% vs. 0.2%), drug-related gastrointestinal adverse reaction (1.3% vs. 0.8%) mainly due to nausea (1.1% vs. 0.4%), thyroid neoplasm (benign) (0.4% vs. 0.0%), and cutaneous skin reactions (0.2% vs. 0.0%). Renal adverse events were reported by more patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group compared to the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (0.8% vs. 0.5%.).
In the safety grouping, 4 patients were reported with pancreatitis. Three of those patients were treated with linagliptin+metformin+insulin, two of which required hospitalisation due to the event; none of the events was reported as having a severe intensity. One patient in the placebo+metformin+insulin group was reported with severe pancreatitis and required hospitalisation due to the event. None of the cases were fatal, necrotising, or haemorrhagic. The risk of pancreatitis was considered to be in line with previous findings with linagliptin and is a listed adverse event in the Summary of Product Characteristics of Jentadueto [R132609]. For further details on adverse events of special interest, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.5.2].

### 5.3.6 Adverse events in subgroups and special populations

#### 5.3.6.1 Age

To determine the effect of age on safety during treatment with linagliptin on a background of metformin and insulin, the following age groups were analysed: less than or 50 years of age (N=160), age between 51 and 64 (N=554), age between 65 and 74 years (N=302), age between 75 and 84 years (N=59), and 85 years or older (N=4). Overall, key patient demographics were comparable between the treatment groups across the analysed age categories. For more information refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.2.1.2].
The proportion of patients with at least one adverse event was generally comparable in the treatment groups in all age categories, except for patients aged 75 to 84 years where more patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group reported adverse events than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and for patients aged 85 years or more, where 2 out of 3 patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group were reported with adverse events and the one patient in the placebo+metformin+insulin group was not reported with adverse events during treatment.
The frequency of adverse events leading to discontinuation of the trial medication was generally higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, except for patients aged 51 to 64 years. The frequency of serious adverse events increased with increasing age, most notably in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. For patients below 64 years, the proportion of patients was higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (up to 14.2%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (up to 9.8%). For older patients, serious adverse events were more frequently reported in the placebo+metformin+insulin group, than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group.
Investigator-reported hypoglycaemic events were more frequent in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group for patients aged 50 years or less and for patients aged 65 to 74 years, and more frequent in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for patients aged 51 to 64 years, and patients aged 75 years or more. Additionally, proportions of patients with investigator-reported hypoglycaemic events were comparable between the treatment groups for patients below 70 years (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 27.9%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 29.1%) and for patients 70 years and above (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 37.2%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 39.8%) [U13-1576, Table 5.3.2.2.10].
Overall, there was no evidence that treatment with linagliptin compared with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin increased the risk for adverse events in patients of different age categories. For more information on adverse events in age subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.1.1].

#### 5.3.6.2 Gender

In the safety grouping, the number of male patients (N=560) was slightly higher than the number of female patients (N=519). Key patient demographics were balanced across the treatment groups, whereas mean baseline weight was higher for male patients and more male patients had normal renal function at baseline compared with female patients [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Tables 3.1.2.2, 3.1.2.8].



Overall, for the proportions of patients with at least one adverse there was no relevant difference between the treatment groups for either gender, except for cardiac disorders which were more common in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for male patients (9.1% vs. 7.3%), and for gastrointestinal disorders which were more common in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for female patients (24.7% vs. 19.5%).
Adverse events leading to discontinuation were reported more frequently for male patients than for female patients, with similar frequencies in both treatment groups. Serious adverse events were more common for male patients (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 16.4%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 15.7%) than for female patients (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 11.0%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 9.8%), with slightly higher frequencies in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. Overall, no meaningful difference was noted between the treatment groups in male and female patients. For more information on adverse events in gender subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.1.2].

#### 5.3.6.3 Race

The majority of patients in the safety grouping were White (N= 891), 142 patients were Asian, and 46 patients were Black. Key demographic data were comparable between the treatment groups in Whites and Asians, except for weight which was lower for Asian patients. For Black patients, mean baseline weight and BMI were higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. Age was lower in Black and Asian patients when compared with White patients [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Table 3.1.2.3].
Overall, the proportion of patients with at least one adverse event was higher in Black patients and Asian patients when compared with White patients, and generally comparable between treatment groups or slightly higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. Results were generally similar within the treatment groups, except for the following SOCs which were more common in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group: gastrointestinal disorders for Asian patients, general disorders and administrative site conditions for Black patients, renal and urinary disorders for White patients, and respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders for Asian patients.
The frequency of patients with adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation was higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for White patients, identical in both treatment groups for Black patients, and higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for Asian patients (7.9% vs. 6.1%). Whereas the frequency of serious adverse events for White and Asian patients was similar in the treatment groups, for Black patients serious adverse events were more often reported for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than for the placebo+metformin+insulin group (30.4% vs. 4.3%). As the number of Black patients was very low in the safety grouping, this numerical difference is not thought to be clinically meaningful. Hence, no meaningful differences between treatment with linagliptin and placebo on a background of metformin and insulin could be detected in the analysed race categories. For more information on adverse events in race subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.1.3].

#### 5.3.6.4 BMI

The key demographic data of both baseline BMI categories (<30 kg/m2, N=480; ≥30 kg/m2, N=599) were similar across the treatment groups. In the subgroup of patients with higher BMI at baseline, there were more female patients, more White patients, and more patients with normal renal function at baseline when compared with the subgroup of patients with lower BMI [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Table 3.1.2.5].
Overall, no relevant differences were noted for the frequency of adverse events in both populations and in the treatment groups. SOCs which were reported more frequently for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than the placebo+metformin+insulin group were cardiac disorders (7.8% vs. 4.6%) and renal and urinary disorders (9.2% vs. 6.3%) for patients with high BMI at baseline, and gastrointestinal disorders (26.4% vs. 18.1%), investigations (12.6% vs. 7.5%), and nervous system disorders (18.9% vs.14.2%) for patients with low BMI at baseline.
The proportion of patients with adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation was slightly higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (5.1%) than the placebo+metformin+insulin group (4.4%) for patients with low BMI, and slightly lower in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (3.4%) than the placebo+metformin+insulin group (4.9%) for patients with high BMI at baseline. The proportion of patients with serious adverse events for patients with low BMI or high BMI at baseline was slightly higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (13.8 and 13.9%, respectively) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (both 12.8%). Generally, there was no indication that treatment with linagliptin compared with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin gave rise to a difference in the safety profile for patients in both BMI categories. For more information on adverse events in BMI subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.1.4].

#### 5.3.6.5 Insulin total daily dose

Most patients in the safety grouping were on a total daily insulin dose of ≤40 IU (N=701), whereas 378 patients were on a total daily insulin dose of >40 IU. The key demographic data of both baseline insulin dose categories were similar across the treatment groups. In the subgroup of lower daily insulin dose, more patients were from Asia and Europe and patients were generally lighter at baseline than patients at a higher daily insulin dose [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Table 3.1.2.9].
Overall, the proportion of patients with at least one adverse event was higher in patients with higher insulin total daily dose than for patients with lower total daily insulin dose, but comparable in the treatment groups. SOCs which were more frequently reported for the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than the placebo+metformin+insulin group were eye disorders for patients on higher daily insulin dose (10.7% vs. 9.0%) and for patients on lower daily insulin dose (8.1% vs. 6.1%), gastrointestinal disorders for patients with higher insulin doses (27.5% vs. 19.0%), general disorders and administrative site conditions for patients with higher insulin doses (14.6% vs. 9.0%), and renal and urinary disorders for patients with higher daily insulin dose (10.7% vs. 8.9%) and for patients with lower daily insulin dose (5.9% vs. 4.2%).
Adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation were more frequently reported for patients with higher daily insulin dose than for patients with lower daily insulin dose, but frequencies were similar in the treatment groups. Serious adverse events were reported for more patients in the high daily insulin dose category (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 16.3%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 14.5%) compared with patients in the low daily insulin dose category (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 12.7%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 11.8%), and were slightly more frequent in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. For patients with higher total daily insulin doses, investigator-reported hypoglycaemic events were more frequent when compared with the patients with lower total daily insulin doses; no relevant difference was noted between the treatment groups. Overall, the frequency of adverse events was higher in the higher total daily insulin category, but there was no evidence that there was a difference in safety between linagliptin and placebo treatment on a background of metformin and insulin. For more information on adverse events in total daily insulin dose subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.1.5].

#### 5.3.6.6 Renal function

Two renal function categories were defined based on baseline eCcr values as calculated using the Cockcroft-Gault formula: normal renal function (eCcr ≥90 mL/min, N= 695) and renal impairment (eCcr <90 mL/min, N= 384). Key demographic characteristics of the renal impairment categories were generally balanced between the treatment groups. In the subgroup of patients with normal renal function, patients were generally younger and heavier, there were more males and White patients than in the subgroup of patients with renal impairment at baseline [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Table 3.1.2.8].
Overall, the proportions of patients with at least one adverse event were higher for patients with renal impairment at baseline when compared with patients with normal renal function, with no relevant differences between the treatment groups.
Adverse events leading to premature discontinuation of the trial drugs were reported for similar proportions of patients with normal renal function in both treatment groups (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 4.6%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 4.0%); for patients with renal impairment the proportion was higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (6.0%) than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (3.5%). The frequency of patients reporting serious adverse events was higher in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (12.6%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (9.8%) for patients with normal renal function, and higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (18.5%) than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (16.0%) for patients with renal impairment at baseline. Overall, adverse events were more common for patients with renal impairment at baseline compared with patients with normal renal function; however, treatment with linagliptin compared with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin did not show meaningful differences in the safety profile. For more information on adverse events in renal function subgroups, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.2.1].

#### 5.3.6.7 Geographical region

Most patients included in the analysis of safety were from Europe (N=523), 250 patients were from South America (including Mexico), 185 patients were from North America (including New Zealand and Australia), and 121 patients were from Asia. Within the geographical regions investigated, the main demographic parameters were generally balanced between treatment groups. In South America, more female patients were included when compared with all other regions. All of the patients in Asia were of Asian race and most of the patients in the other regions were White [U13-1576, Section 2.2.2 and Table 3.1.2.4].
Overall, the proportions of patients with at least one adverse event were lower in Europe than in all other regions; proportions were comparable between treatment groups, except for Asia, where there was a higher frequency of patients with adverse events in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group. On the SOC level, adverse events which were more frequently reported in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were eye disorders in South America, gastrointestinal disorders, psychiatric disorders, respiratory and thoracic and mediastinal disorders, and neoplasms in Asia, investigations and vascular disorders in North America, renal and urinary disorders in Europe and North America.
Adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation were more frequently reported in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (7.7%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (5.4%) for patients from Asia, were reported in similar frequencies for patients from North America, and were reported more frequently in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for patients from Europe and South America. Serious adverse events were more frequently reported in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for patients from Asia (20.0% vs. 17.9%), Europe (14.2% vs. 13.0%), and South America (11.6% vs. 7.8%); they were more common in patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group for patients from North America. The majority of patients in the safety grouping was from Europe and no meaningful treatment differences were noted for these patients. For the other regions, the number of patients included in the safety grouping was relatively small; however, no evident trend for a change in safety was found when comparing linagliptin and placebo treatment on a background of metformin and insulin in these patient populations. For more information on adverse events in the analysed geographical regions, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.2.1].

### 5.3.7 Cardiovascular safety analysis

An independent Clinical Event Committee (CEC) adjudicated all potential events in a blinded manner for the Phase III clinical programme. In line with regulatory requirements, an update of the formal pre-specified cardiovascular (CV) meta-analysis of linagliptin was performed with a data cut-off in August 2012. This analysis included all Phase III and one Phase IIb study in the Boehringer Ingelheim project database for linagliptin; the number of patients who were analysed for the primary endpoint in this meta-analysis were 5847 patients treated with linagliptin, 2675 patients treated with placebo, and 937 patients treated with an active comparator. The primary composite endpoint was composed of the adjudicated events CV death, non-fatal myocardial ischaemia (MI), non-fatal stroke, and hospitalisation due to unstable angina; the secondary endpoints were the composite of the adjudicated events CV death, non-fatal MI, and non-fatal stroke, the composite of all adjudicated events, and the FDA-defined custom major adverse CV events (MACE) derived from 34 MedDRA preferred terms; tertiary endpoints were all of the individual adjudicated components and total mortality. For all endpoints analysed, the meta-analysis confirmed that treatment with linagliptin does not increase CV risk against a pooled comparator group of placebo, glimepiride or voglibose in patients with or without background therapy. For further information, refer to [U12-2369].
All CEC-confirmed events reported for the safety grouping analysed for this application were reported in study 1218.36. As the analysis was not powered to show differences in cardiovascular events in the treatment groups, the following results are considered exploratory. For the safety analysis in this application, the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint as defined for the meta-analysis was analysed. Of the 549 treated patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, 14 patients (2.6%) had CEC-confirmed events as defined by this endpoint; giving rise to an incidence rate of 21.3 patients with events per 1000 patient years (PY) at risk. Of the 530 treated patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group, 10 patients (1.9%) had such events, leading to an incidence rate of 16.2/1000 PY. The imbalance was mainly driven by results for CV death (3 patients with events in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group vs. no patients with events in the placebo+metformin+insulin group), and non-fatal MI (4 patients with events in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group vs. 2 patients with events in the placebo+metformin+insulin group). All events were reported for very low numbers of patients. Furthermore, all patients reported with CV death also had concomitant diagnoses which are risk factors for cardiovascular complications. Non-fatal stroke and hospitalisation due to unstable angina were reported with similar frequencies in the treatment groups.
A second endpoint was analysed for this application based on FDA custom MACE events and included the preferred terms as listed in the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 1.1.3.2]. For this endpoint, number of patients with events and incidence rates were similar in the treatment groups: in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, 6 patients (1.1%) were reported with such events (incidence rate: 9.1/1000 PY); in the placebo+metformin+insulin group, 5 patients were reported with such events (incidence rate: 8.1/1000 PY).
Considering the results noted in the recent meta-analysis of CV events in the linagliptin program and the results of the primary and secondary endpoint analysed for this application in a subgroup of patients on metformin and insulin background, there does not appear to be a relevant increase in cardiovascular risk for patients treated with linagliptin+metformin+insulin. The CV safety profile of the triple combination linagliptin, metformin, and insulin is considered to be in line with the currently known overall CV safety data for linagliptin. For further details on the cardiovascular safety analysis, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.1.2.3].

### 5.3.8 Analysis of adverse events over long-term treatment

For diabetes medication, the analysis of safety over long-term treatment is requested by regulatory guidelines. Therefore, the analysis of safety in the SCS included data from trials with a treatment exposure of 52 weeks and above. A substantial proportion of patients in the safety grouping had treatment exposure of more than 1 year (82.3% of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, 77.7% of patients in the placebo+metformin+insulin group), and many patients were reported with treatment exposure of more than 88 weeks (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: 8.4%, placebo+metformin+insulin: 10.6%). Hence, analyses carried out in the safety grouping and described in the SCS [Module 2.7.4] are considered sufficient in order to evaluate safety in patients over long-term treatment. Overall, no meaningful change of the safety profile is expected when comparing short and long-term exposure with linagliptin on a background of metformin and insulin compared to exposure with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin.

### 5.3.9 Summary of identified side effects

For the analysis of safety of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin in this application, an analysis of the identified side effects for linagliptin+metformin was performed in the safety grouping. No new adverse events or changes in severity associated with the use of linagliptin+metformin with insulin add-on were identified. For further information, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4; Section 2.1.7].

## 5.4 CLINICAL LABORATORY EVALUATION AND VITAL SIGNS

### 5.4.1 Clinical laboratory evaluation

Laboratory data were analysed by descriptive statistics, transitions relative to reference range at baseline and last value on treatment, and possibly clinically significant abnormalities. In addition, liver enzyme elevations including potential Hy’s law cases, and analysis of changes in renal function were investigated. For detailed results of the clinically laboratory analysis, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 3].
In the safety grouping, no clinically meaningful changes from baseline or clinically meaningful differences between the treatment groups were observed for haematology parameters, electrolytes, enzymes, substrates, and urinalysis parameters. Of note were changes in mean (SD) amylase values; they increased from baseline by 5 (20) U/L in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group until the end of treatment, whereas in the placebo+metformin+insulin group a change by -1 (21) U/L was noted. Furthermore, for amylase a higher proportion of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (4.5%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (2.9%) shifted from normal values at baseline to high values at the end of treatment. Analyses for possibly clinically significant abnormalities of increased amylase confirmed the above mentioned findings, with more patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group (5.6%) than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group (3.3%). The reported adverse events did not suggest that the noted changes in amylase and the differences between the treatment groups were of clinical relevance.
Only few patients were reported with any of the pre-specified criteria for liver enzyme elevations, most commonly they were from the placebo+metformin+insulin group. One patient in the placebo+metformin+insulin group and no patient in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group had a lab constellation consistent with potential Hy's law. Mean changes from baseline to last value on treatment were small for all liver enzymes analysed; no relevant differences were noted for the treatment groups. However, shifts from low or normal values at baseline towards high values at the end of treatment were more common in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group for AST (5.8% vs. 2.7%) and ALT (4.6% vs. 3.2%). Considering additionally the result for hepatic adverse events, presented as adverse events of special interest in Section 5.3.5, a slight imbalance was noted as more patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were reported with hepatic events. As most of these events in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group were mild or moderate in intensity, led to discontinuation in only one case, and were considered serious for only one patient, it is considered that the noted imbalances in hepatic enzymes and hepatic adverse events have no relevant clinical impact on the safety profile of linagliptin+metformin as an add-on to insulin.
Shifts from normal renal function at baseline to mild renal impairment and from mild renal impairment at baseline to moderate renal impairment at the end of treatment were noted for more patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. The frequency of patients with observed shifts in urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio categories were comparable in the treatment groups.

### 5.4.2 Vital signs

Evaluations of vital signs included descriptive statistics, shifts of vitals signs parameters in regard to reference ranges, and the analysis of marked increases and marked outliers for pulse rate, systolic, and diastolic blood pressure. Clinically relevant abnormal findings in 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) measurements and in the physical examination were to be reported as adverse events.
When observing timepoints beyond Week 18 until Week 91, for patients from study 1218.36 only, mean SBP slightly decreased (by a maximum of -3.2 mmHg from baseline at Week 65) in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group, whereas mean SBP slightly increased towards the end of the treatment (by a maximum of 2.9 mmHg from baseline at Week 91) in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. For mean DBP, slight decreases from baseline to the end of treatment were noted (linagliptin+metformin+insulin: -1.0 mmHg, placebo+metformin+insulin: -0.9 mmHg).

Mean changes in pulse rate from baseline until the end of treatment were negligible in both treatment groups. Proportions of patients with marked increases and marked outliers were small and comparable in the treatment groups. None of the patients in the safety grouping was reported with an adverse event of the PT 'electrocardiogram abnormal'. Overall, no meaningful differences were noted between patients treated with linagliptin and patients treated with placebo on a background of metformin and insulin. Further details on the analysis of vital signs are provided in the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 4].

## 5.5 SAFETY IN SPECIAL SITUATIONS

Information on metformin is given in the summary of product characteristics of metformin [R10-5537, R11-0761].

### 5.5.1 Pregnancy and lactation

No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women were undertaken for this submission or are available. Because animal reproduction studies are not always predictive of human response, linagliptin like other antidiabetic medications, should be avoided during pregnancy.
Pregnant and nursing mothers were excluded from the studies in the linagliptin clinical development programme. In all studies included in this SCS, females of child-bearing potential participating in the study were asked to take appropriate contraceptive precautions to prevent becoming pregnant during the study. All women with child-bearing potential had to undergo pregnancy testing at screening.
Only one patient from study 1218.36 included in this safety grouping was reported with pregnancy during the study. The patient was on insulin and metformin background medication and was randomised to placebo treatment. At 34 gestational weeks, the pregnancy ended with a live birth (birth weight 2000 grams; Apgar score 2). For further details, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 5.4].
It is unknown whether linagliptin is excreted in human breast milk. Animal studies have shown excretion of linagliptin in breast milk, and linagliptin should therefore not be taken during breast feeding.

### 5.5.2 Overdose

In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in healthy male subjects, single doses of linagliptin of up to 600 mg (equivalent to 120 tablets) were administered orally (as a tablet) and were generally well tolerated. The occurrence of adverse events was not dose dependent. Neither clinically relevant changes of laboratory parameters nor of ECG parameters were observed (BI study 1218.1) [U05-2072]. Based on linagliptin’s mode of action and on its pharmacokinetic characteristics, it is unlikely that acute overdosing of linagliptin will affect vital functions or cause life-threatening hypoglycaemia. In the event of an overdose, the usual supportive measures should be employed, e.g. removal of unabsorbed material from the gastrointestinal tract, clinical monitoring, and institution of supportive therapy if required.

### 5.5.3 Drug abuse, withdrawal, and rebound

Linagliptin is not a controlled substance and has not been systematically studied in animals or humans for its potential for abuse, tolerance, or physical dependence. In the studies analysed for the evaluation of safety in this application, no potential for abuse of linagliptin was observed. Since the pharmacological properties, as well as non-clinical and clinical data do not indicate an impact on the central nervous system suggestive for stimulant, depressant, hallucinogenic, or mood-elevating effects, the potential for abuse is believed to be minimal.
In the studies analysed for this application, there were no withdrawal or rebound phenomena in any of the patients.

### 5.5.4 EFFECTS ON ABILITY TO DRIVE OR OPERATE MACHINERY OR IMPAIRMENT OF MENTAL ABILITY

No studies on the effects on the ability to drive and use machines have been performed.

## 5.6 POST-MARKETING EXPERIENCE

Jentadueto® was first authorised on 30 Jan 2012 in the United States. At data cut-off for the evaluation of post-marketing experience, Jentadueto was authorised in 40 countries. The overall cumulative marketed patient exposure to Jentadueto is estimated to be 9008 PY for the time period 30 Jan 2012 to 31 Dec 2012. The first Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR) for Jentadueto® covers a reporting interval of 20 Jul 2012 to 19 Jan 2013. The interval patient exposure was estimated to be 6740 patient years [U13-1444].
The post-marketing experience presented here summarises all spontaneous cases (spontaneous reports, health authorities, observational studies, registries, literature) received for Jentadueto from health professionals and non-health professionals as well as serious cases from clinical trials up to 19 Jan 2013. The source for the data evaluation was the Boehringer Ingelheim Global Drug Safety Database.
In total, 46 cases (confirmed by healthcare professionals and non-confirmed cases) were reported, of which 3 cases were serious and 43 cases were non-serious. None of the cases were assessed as requiring a change to the safety profile as described in the company core data sheet (CCDS dated 14 December 2012). The majority of the non-serious cases appeared to describe listed adverse events such as gastrointestinal upset, rash, or cough, or events that are associated with diabetes such as hypoglycaemia and peripheral neuropathy. The serious cases were one case of lactic acidosis, one poorly reported case of hypokalaemia, and one case of rhabdomyolysis in a patient with concomitant simvastatin medication. Overall, no case of pancreatitis was reported from spontaneous sources until datalock point of the PSUR.


Since the datalock point for this post marketing evaluation, 2 cases have been received that are under close surveillance in the Jentadueto Risk Management Plan (version 5, 21 Mar 2013 ); one case of pancreatitis and a serious case of erythema multiforme. For both cases, laboratory measurements and concomitant medication of the patients offered alternative explanations.
In summary, the current benefit risk assessment for Jentadueto remains unchanged and positive. For further details on the post-marketing experience with linagliptin+metformin, please refer to the Periodic Safety Update Report [U13-1444]. For further information on the cases described above, refer to the SCS [Module 2.7.4, Section 6].

# 6. BENEFITS AND RISKS CONCLUSIONS

This application is based on an evaluation of the efficacy and safety of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin compared with the combination of placebo, metformin, and insulin in patients with type 2 diabetes and includes patients from three Phase III placebo-controlled clinical trials. From these trials, only those patients taking both metformin and insulin as background therapy were included. The efficacy evaluation is primarily based on a subset of patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36, in which linagliptin was investigated in addition to a background of basal insulin therapy with or without oral antidiabetic drugs. Further proof of efficacy is provided by patients from a trial in which linagliptin was investigated in elderly patients (1218.63). The analysis of safety was mainly based on patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36 and the supportive trial 1218.63; an additional 3 patients from the renal impairment trial 1218.43 are also included.
---
**Efficacy**
In patients from the pivotal trial 1218.36 (i.e. EFF-1), the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin provided a clinically meaningful reduction in HbA~1c~ from baseline compared with the combination of placebo, metformin, and insulin. The adjusted mean treatment difference in HbA~1c~ was -0.68% after 24 weeks, which is consistent with that (-0.64%) reported for the combination of linagliptin and metformin compared with placebo and metformin in the pivotal trial 1218.17 [U09-2533] from the linagliptin monotherapy programme. In elderly patients aged 70 years and above (i.e. EFF-2), the adjusted mean treatment difference in HbA~1c~ from baseline between the treatment groups was -0.81% after 24 weeks. For both efficacy groupings studied, the results for HbA~1c~ were corroborated by the analyses of proportion of patients attaining HbA~1c~ of less than 7% and lowering of at least 0.5%, reduction of fasting plasma glucose and proportion of patients requiring rescue medication. The persistence of efficacy was based on data up to 52 weeks for EFF-1 and showed a sustained clinically meaningful treatment effect over time with regard to HbA~1c~ reduction from baseline. Beyond 24 weeks, when investigators were free to adjust the insulin dose, only minimal changes in mean insulin dose were seen for both treatment groups.  Moreover, only minimal changes in body weight were observed, confirming that linagliptin is weight-neutral.

For most subgroups of EFF-1 analysed, the treatment difference was consistent between the combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin and the combination of placebo, metformin, and insulin. The triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin provided clinically meaningful improvements in glycaemic control in all geographical regions, including Europe which provided the largest proportion of patients.
---
**Safety**
The safety data of this application is based on an exposure of 549 patients to linagliptin 5 mg and 530 patients to placebo; all patients were on a background of metformin and insulin. In regard to patient disposition, the frequencies of premature discontinuation were higher in the placebo+metformin+insulin group than in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group; this was consistent across most subgroups.
The overall frequencies of treatment-emergent adverse events were comparable between the treatment groups, as were the frequencies of adverse events leading to premature treatment discontinuation and severe adverse events. A slightly higher proportion of patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group reported serious adverse events when compared with the placebo+metformin+insulin group. However, differences in frequencies of serious adverse events on PT level which were more common in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group did not exceed 0.4%. On the PT level, no serious event was reported with a frequency of more than 1 %. Incidence rates of fatal events were similar in the treatment groups. With regard to subgroups, no meaningful differences in incidences of adverse events were noted comparing patients treated with linagliptin or placebo on a background of metformin and insulin. Also for hypoglycaemic events, there was no evidence that linagliptin treatment increased the frequency of events compared with placebo treatment on a background of metformin and insulin.
A number of adverse events of special interest were analysed for which the overall frequencies were generally low in both treatment groups. The adverse events of special interest reported with an at least 0.5% higher frequency in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group than in the placebo+metformin+insulin group were hepatic adverse events, hypersensitivity adverse events (mainly due to the urticaria), and drug-related gastrointestinal adverse reaction (mainly due to nausea). Pancreatitis was reported for three patients in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and one patient in the placebo+metformin+insulin group. None of the cases were fatal, necrotising, or haemorrhagic, and the events in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group were not of severe intensity. The risk of pancreatitis was considered to be in line with previous findings with linagliptin and other DDP-4 inhibitors. No patients were reported with AESIs defined as pancreatic cancer, thyroid cancer, or thyroid neoplasm (unspecified); 'thyroid neoplasm (benign)' was reported for 2 patients (0.4%) in the linagliptin+metformin+insulin group and no patient in the placebo+metformin+insulin group.
Overall, the safety laboratory data revealed no clear trends of clinical relevance for the combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin. Imbalances noted for the laboratory analysis of amylase and hepatic enzymes do not impact on the overall positive safety profile of the triple combination linagliptin+metformin+insulin. There were no clinically relevant differences in vital signs (blood pressure, pulse rate) between the treatment groups.
No new side effects or changes in severity to already known side effects for linagliptin monotherapy or linagliptin+metformin were found to be associated with the use of linagliptin+metformin with insulin add-on.
---
**Overall conclusion**
In patients with type 2 diabetes, treatment with the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin was well tolerated and was superior to the combination of placebo, metformin, and insulin in the reduction of HbA~1c~ and fasting plasma glucose levels. The treatment effect is considered clinically meaningful and was achieved without an excess of hypoglycaemia. The information presented demonstrates a positive benefit to risk ratio of the triple combination of linagliptin, metformin, and insulin, including for elderly patients. The data support the use of the fixed-dose combination of linagliptin and metformin as combination therapy with insulin in adult patients with type 2 diabetes when insulin and metformin do not provide adequate glycaemic control.
